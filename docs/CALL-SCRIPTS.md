# Call Scripts — Horry County

How to work every lead from ApexForge sites. Written for a part-time schedule: the form does the interviewing, so your call is short and starts with an offer.

> **Placeholders:** `{Your Name}`, `{Agency}`, `{Brand}` (the website name), `{Partner}`, `{Brokerage}`, `{City}`, `{callback number}`. Words in *italics* are notes to you, not things to say.

---

## Part 0 — Rules for every call (read once, follow always)

### South Carolina calling law (Telephone Privacy Protection Act, S.C. Code §37-21)

- **Hours:** call only between **8:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m.** in the lead's time zone (§37-21-30).
- **At the start of every call, say:** your **first and last name**, the **business you're calling for**, and **why you're calling** — then give your **callback number** early in the call (§37-21-40).
- **If they say stop, stop.** End the call politely, set the lead to **dnc** in the admin, and never call or text that number again. SC requires honoring that for at least **5 years** (§37-21-70).
- **Caller ID:** show a real number you can be reached at. Don't spoof numbers.
- **Penalties are real:** $1,000 per violation, up to $5,000 if willful, plus attorney fees (§37-21-80).

Your web leads checked a consent box naming you and asked to be contacted, which is the permission that makes these calls allowed. Still:

- If a lead is **more than 3 months old**, scrub it against the National Do Not Call Registry before calling (free for up to 5 area codes at telemarketing.donotcall.gov).
- Only offer what the consent box covered. The admin shows the exact consent text on every lead.

### Things never to do

- Never ask for or write down a **Social Security number or bank details** in notes, texts, email or the admin. Those go **only** into the carrier's secure e-application.
- On real-estate and mortgage leads: you are not licensed in those fields. **Don't give price opinions, rate quotes or loan advice.** Gather facts and introduce your partner.
- Don't promise approval, a specific rate, or "guaranteed" anything until a carrier has said so.

### Why speed matters (and how to get it with a full-time job)

A study of 15,000+ web leads (Oldroyd, Lead Response Management Study) found that calling within **5 minutes** instead of 30 gave about **100× better odds of reaching** the person and **21× better odds of qualifying** them. You can't always call in 5 minutes, so:

1. **Booking link:** put a free Cal.com or Google Calendar booking link in each site's settings. After submitting, leads pick a time — they expect your call, and it fits your schedule.
2. **Instant alert:** set up the Discord/Slack notification (Settings) so leads hit your phone immediately.
3. **60-second text** when you can't call yet (template T1 below). A fast human reply beats a perfect late call.
4. **Batch calls** at lunch and 5:30–8:30 p.m., plus Saturday mornings.

### Follow-up cadence (if you don't reach them)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 0 | Call → voicemail **V1** → text **T1** |
| 1 | Call (morning) and call (evening) |
| 2 | Text **T2** |
| 3 | Call → voicemail **V2** |
| 5 | Call + text **T3** |
| 8 | Final text **T4**, then set status **contacted**, note "no answer" |

Stop immediately if they reply STOP or ask you not to contact them.

## Part 1 — Life insurance (you call these and close them)

### Before you dial (2 minutes)

1. Open the lead in **Admin → Leads**. Read the **Quote sheet** and the **underwriting read** (preferred / level / graded / guaranteed-issue).
2. Click **Copy** and paste into your quoting tool. *(No paid quoting tool yet? Ask your IMO or upline; most give contracted agents a free quote engine.)*
3. Pick **three options** that fit their stated budget: a lower amount, the amount they asked for, and a little more. Write the monthly price for each.
4. Glance at **Still to gather on the call** so nothing's missed.

### The offer call

**Open (SC-compliant, ~20 seconds)**

> "Hi, is this {first name}? Hi {first name}, this is **{Your Name}** with **{Agency}**. I'm a licensed life insurance agent here in Horry County. I'm calling because you asked for life insurance quotes on **{Brand}** {today / on Tuesday}, and I've got your prices ready. My number is **{callback number}** in case we get cut off. Do you have about 10 minutes now?"

- *No time:* "No problem — what's better, later today or tomorrow evening?" Book it, send **T1**.
- *"Who is this / how did you get my number?"* → see Objection 6.

**Confirm (1–2 minutes).** Quotes are only good if the answers are right.

> "Before I show you numbers, let me make sure I have everything right. You were born on {DOB}, about {height} and {weight}, and {no tobacco in the last year}. Still accurate?"

> "You mentioned {condition}. When were you diagnosed, and what do you take for it now? Any hospital stays for it in the last couple of years?" *(Repeat for each condition. If something changes the underwriting class, re-quote before presenting.)*

> "And this is mainly to {their reason from the form: cover final expenses / protect the mortgage / replace your income} — is that right? Who would you want this to go to?"

**Present (2–3 minutes)**

> "Based on that, I checked {several} carriers. Here are the three that fit what you told me:
> - **Option 1:** ${amount} of coverage for **${price} a month**.
> - **Option 2:** ${amount} for **${price} a month** — this is the one closest to what you asked for.
> - **Option 3:** ${amount} for **${price} a month**.
>
> Which of those feels right for you?"

Then stop talking and let them answer.

*Use the line that matches their reason:*

- **Final expense:** "The point of this is that nobody in your family has to scramble to pay for a funeral. The National Funeral Directors Association put the median cost of a funeral with viewing and burial around $8,300 in its 2023 survey — this covers that and the other bills that come with it."
- **Mortgage protection:** "If something happened to you, would your family want to stay in the house? This is sized so they could pay it off or keep making payments."
- **Term / income:** "If your paycheck stopped tomorrow, how many years would your family need to get back on their feet? That's what the term length is for."
- **Graded or guaranteed-issue:** "Because of {condition}, this plan has a waiting period: if something happens in the first {2} years, your family gets {back the premiums paid plus interest / a portion}; after that, the full amount. Accidents are usually covered from day one. *(Check the carrier's exact terms.)*"

**Close (start the application)**

> "Great choice. The application takes about 15–20 minutes, and many carriers give a decision quickly, sometimes the same day. I'll walk you through it. You'll need:
> - your beneficiary's full name and date of birth,
> - your doctor's name,
> - and the bank account you'd like the monthly payment to come from. You'll enter those details inside the insurance company's secure application — I'll never ask you to read them to me in a text or email.
>
> Would you like to pick a draft date that lines up with when you get paid?"

*Then work through the **Still to gather on the call** checklist and the carrier's e-app. Afterwards set status **sold** in the admin.*

**After the sale (30 seconds — this is how you get free leads)**

> "Thanks for trusting me with this. Most of my clients come from people like you. Is there anyone in your family — parents, grown kids — who'd want me to check prices for them too?"

### Life objections

**1. "I need to think about it."**
> "Of course. Usually that means either the price or the amount isn't quite right. Which one is it for you?" *(Adjust the amount to fit the budget; offer to hold the quote until a set time tomorrow.)*

**2. "I need to talk to my spouse."**
> "That makes sense — it's a family decision. Is your spouse there now? I'm happy to go over it together. If not, what time tonight works for a quick three-way call?"

**3. "It's too expensive."**
> "Let's make it fit. What monthly amount would feel comfortable?" *(Re-quote a lower amount. Something in force beats nothing.)*

**4. "I already have coverage through work."**
> "That's great to have. Two things to know: work coverage usually ends when you leave or retire, and it's often only one or two years of salary. This would be yours no matter where you work. Want me to show you how much to add on top of what you have?"

**5. "Just mail me something / email me."**
> "I can email a summary right now. The prices depend on your health and age, though, and they go up every birthday. If the numbers look good, are you open to starting the application today?"

**6. "How did you get my number? / Is this a scam?"**
> "Totally fair to ask. You filled out the form on **{Brand}** on {date} asking for life insurance quotes, and you asked to be contacted at this number. I'm {Your Name}, licensed in South Carolina — you can look up my license on the SC Department of Insurance website. If you'd rather I not call, I'll take you off my list right now."

**7. "I'm not interested anymore."**
> "No problem at all, thanks for letting me know. I'll take you off my list." *(Set status **dnc**. Don't push.)*

### Voicemails (under 20 seconds)

- **V1:** "Hi {first name}, it's {Your Name} with {Agency}, a licensed life insurance agent here in Horry County. You asked for life insurance quotes on {Brand} — I have your prices ready. Call or text me at {number}. Again, {number}. Thanks!"
- **V2:** "Hi {first name}, {Your Name} again with {Agency}. Your quotes are ready and I don't want them to expire. Text me a good time to call: {number}."

### Texts

Every first text includes your name, business and the opt-out.

- **T1 (within minutes):** "Hi {first name}, this is {Your Name} with {Agency} — got your quote request from {Brand}. I'm a licensed agent here in Horry County. When's a good time for a 10-minute call? Reply STOP to opt out."
- **T2:** "Hi {first name}, {Your Name} here. Your life insurance quotes are ready. Would today at lunch or this evening work better for a quick call?"
- **T3:** "Hi {first name}, still happy to go over your quotes — or book any time that suits you here: {booking link}"
- **T4:** "Hi {first name}, I'll close out your quote request for now. If you'd still like prices, just reply here or call {number}. Take care!"

## Part 2 — Real estate leads (check quality, then hand off to your realtor)

**Your role:** you're the friendly first call. Confirm the lead is real and serious, then introduce your partner. **You don't give price opinions or real-estate advice** — that's licensed activity.

**Open**

> "Hi, is this {first name}? Hi, this is **{Your Name}** with **{Your S-Corp}**. I'm calling because you asked about {selling your home / your home's value / buying a home} on **{Brand}**. My number is **{callback number}**. Do you have two minutes?"

**Confirm and qualify (3 minutes).** The form already has most of this; confirm it and fill the gaps.

*Sellers:*
1. "I have the property at {address}. Is that right?"
2. "What's got you thinking about selling?" *(Listen — this is the most useful thing for your partner.)*
3. "When would you ideally like to be moved?"
4. "Is it currently listed with an agent?" *(If yes and still active: "Thanks — since you're already working with someone, I won't step on that." Mark **bad**, note "listed". Don't introduce.)*
5. "Anything about the home a buyer should know — repairs, updates?"
6. "Do you know roughly what you still owe on it?" *(Optional — only if they're comfortable.)*

*Buyers:*
1. "Which areas are you looking at?"
2. "Have you been pre-approved for a mortgage yet, or paying cash?"
3. "Do you need to sell a home before you buy?"
4. "Are you working with an agent already?" *(If yes, stop here — same as above.)*

**Hand off (get permission first — this is what lets your partner contact them)**

> "Thanks, this is really helpful. I work with **{Partner}** at **{Brokerage}**, who's a licensed REALTOR and does a lot of business in {City}. They can {run a market analysis on your home / set up showings} and answer all the pricing questions. **Is it okay if I share your information with {Partner} and have them call you?** What's the best time?"

*Only after a clear "yes":*
1. *In the admin, open the lead and add a note like: **"Verbal OK on {date} {time} to introduce {Partner}, {Brokerage}."** That note is your proof of permission, because your partner wasn't named on the website form.*
2. *Best option: a warm three-way call right now — "I can get them on the line now if that's easier."* Otherwise text {Partner} the summary.
3. *Set **Buyer** to the partner's name and status **sold** (or **qualified** until they accept it).*

*If they say no, or want to think about it: don't pass their information on. Note it, and follow up yourself.*

**Optional cross-offer** *(only if the consent text on the lead covers mortgage protection — the admin shows it):*
> "One more thing — I'm also a licensed life insurance agent. When you're settled into the new place, I help homeowners make sure the mortgage would be paid off if something happened. Want me to send you a quick quote down the road?"

## Part 3 — Mortgage leads (check quality, then hand off to your loan officer)

**Your role:** same as real estate. **Never quote rates, payments or approval odds** — that's the loan officer's job and is regulated.

**Open**

> "Hi, is this {first name}? Hi, this is **{Your Name}** with **{Your S-Corp}**. You asked about {a home loan / refinancing / a home equity line} on **{Brand}**. My number is **{callback number}**. Got two minutes?"

**Confirm and qualify**

1. "You're looking at a home around {price range} — is that right?" *(Refi: "and you owe about {balance}?")*
2. "Have you found a home yet, or are you still looking?" *(Under contract = hot: hand off the same day.)*
3. "What's your timeline?"
4. "Is it a primary home, second home or investment?"
5. "Have you or your spouse served in the military?" *(VA may be an option — mention it to the loan officer.)*
6. "Anything going on with your credit or income the lender should know about? No details needed — just so they come prepared."

**Hand off (get permission first)**

> "Perfect. I work with **{Partner}**, a licensed loan officer with **{Company}** (NMLS #{number}). They'll go over rates and what you qualify for — I don't quote those myself. **Is it okay if I share your information with {Partner} and have them call you?** What time's best?"

*Only after a clear "yes": add the note **"Verbal OK on {date} {time} to introduce {Partner}, {Company}"**, do a warm three-way call if you can (or text {Partner} the summary), then set Buyer and status.*

**Optional cross-offer** *(if consent covers it):* same as real estate — offer a mortgage protection quote after closing.

## Part 4 — Pitching your realtor and lender partners

You already know these people. Keep it short and specific.

> "Hey {name}, quick one. I've built local websites for Horry County that bring in people who want to sell, buy or get a mortgage. Every lead fills out a detailed form, agrees to be contacted, and I personally call them first to make sure they're real and serious before anyone gets passed along.
>
> I'd like to send you the first **5 verified leads free** so you can judge the quality yourself. If you like them, we set a flat price per verified lead, with a weekly cap you choose. If a lead turns out to be a bad number or a duplicate, I replace it. Interested?"

**Get from each partner before launch:**
- Exact name, brokerage/company and license or NMLS number (for your intro script now, and for the consent box later if you ever let them contact leads directly)
- License number (SC real estate license or NMLS)
- Which cities/price ranges they want, and their weekly cap
- Best way to receive leads (text, email) and how fast they'll call (ask for same-day)

**Compliance notes for partners (confirm before charging):**
- **Real estate:** South Carolina generally bars licensees from paying unlicensed people for *referrals*. A flat marketing fee per lead that doesn't depend on a closing is the usual structure, but have your partner confirm it with their **broker-in-charge** (and ideally a SC real-estate attorney) before you invoice anyone.
- **Mortgage:** RESPA Section 8 prohibits paying for referrals of settlement business. A flat, fair-market fee per lead that isn't tied to a loan closing is the standard approach; the lender's compliance team will want to review it.
- **Life insurance referrals back to you:** realtors and lenders can refer clients to you for mortgage protection, but don't pay them per policy (paying insurance commissions to unlicensed people is prohibited). Reciprocal referrals are fine.

## Part 5 — Judging lead quality (your first 30–50 leads)

You're calling everything first to see quality. Track it in **Admin → Leads** with statuses and notes, and review weekly:

| Metric | How to count | Healthy early target |
|---|---|---|
| Contact rate | Reached a real person ÷ all leads | 50%+ |
| Accurate info | Name/phone/details matched the form | 85%+ |
| Qualified | Real need + timeline under 6 months (RE/mortgage) or wants quotes (life) | 40%+ of contacted |
| Appointment / handoff accepted | Agreed to app (life) or intro (RE/mortgage) | 25%+ of contacted |
| Life close rate | Applications submitted ÷ contacted | 10–20% |

If a source or city page brings junk, tell me which one and we'll tighten that page or the form.
