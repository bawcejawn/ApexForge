Final expense insurance is a small whole life policy, usually between about $5,000 and $35,000, meant to cover a funeral and the bills that come with it. The National Funeral Directors Association's 2023 study put the national median cost of a funeral with viewing and burial at $8,300, and $6,280 for a funeral with cremation. That's before medical bills, a final credit card balance or travel for family. Final expense coverage is designed so your family doesn't have to pay those costs out of pocket.

## Who it's a good fit for

Final expense policies are most popular with people roughly 50 to 85 who:

- don't have life insurance anymore, often because a work policy ended at retirement,
- have savings they'd rather leave to family than spend on a funeral,
- have health conditions that make larger policies hard to get.

## How it works

Most final expense policies are **whole life**. Coverage lasts your whole life as long as premiums are paid, the premium generally stays the same, and the policy builds a small cash value. Many carriers use **simplified issue** underwriting: a set of health questions and a prescription history check, with no medical exam.

## How your health affects your options

Carriers sort applicants into roughly three groups. Which group you land in depends on the carrier, so comparing several matters.

- **Level (immediate) coverage:** the full benefit from day one. Many people with common, well-managed conditions such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol or diabetes on pills still qualify with the right carrier.
- **Graded or modified coverage:** for some recent or more serious conditions. If death from illness happens in the first two or three years, the policy typically pays back premiums plus interest or a portion of the benefit. Accidental death is often covered in full from the start. Terms vary by carrier.
- **Guaranteed issue:** no health questions. These usually have a waiting period and cost more for the same amount of coverage, so they're best saved for when other options aren't available.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Applying to the first carrier you see on TV.** Many advertised plans are guaranteed issue, which may cost more than a plan you'd qualify for elsewhere.
- **Leaving out a health condition.** Insurers check prescription and medical records. An inaccurate application can lead to a denied claim.
- **Buying too little.** Think about the funeral you'd want, plus any bills your family would need to settle.
